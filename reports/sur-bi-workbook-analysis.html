SUR Group · Research and planning brief

SUR BI: from the teams’ KPI workbook to a group-wide performance platform

Assessment: the workbook is a useful business requirements baseline, but not yet an implementation-ready KPI contract. The ambition is wider than a dashboard: it combines performance reporting, accountability, assurance, recovery actions and controlled Board reporting.

Prepared 13 October 2026. Primary source: Business Intelligence Platform Development Sept 26 workbook, especially Dashboard 1. Supporting review: KPI Register, Dashboard, Lists, Developer Specification and selected existing SUR BI code.

Evidence boundary. This workbook contains no entered performance observations in its six-period register, no entered targets and no named KPI owners. 1. What the organisation is trying to build

The intended operating model is: a Local Business Manager or delegate supplies or reviews a result; a Head of Department assures it; Operations compares offices and intervenes; the Board sees approved performance, risk and action summaries. This is supported by scoped access, versioned targets, evidence, audit history and period locking.

The valuable outcome is not merely putting five systems on one screen. It is producing one trusted view of demand, workforce capacity, care delivery, financial outcomes and compliance, with explanations and owners when results differ from expectations.

2. The definitions that determine whether the numbers can be trusted

Every metric needs an approved name, purpose, business owner, source, grain, reporting calendar, numerator/denominator where applicable, exclusions, direction, aggregation method, target, thresholds, refresh expectations and access classification.

MeasureRecommended definition / decision required
Revenue and Growth %Distinguish operational invoiced revenue from finance-approved recognised revenue. Define VAT, credit notes, accruals and comparable periods. Growth = change ÷ previous comparable value; previous zero needs an explicit display rule.
Invoiced Hours / Average Client ValueSeparate hour-based invoice lines from day-rate or non-care charges. Client value needs a defined period and distinct billable-client population.
Enquiry → consultation → startCapture dated stage events. Cohort conversion measures the outcome of the same enquiry cohort; starts this month ÷ enquiries this month is a different period-throughput ratio and can be misleading because of lag.
Total Clients / Net Client GrowthDefine active at period end versus receiving care during the period. Net growth must have an agreed policy for new starts, terminations, reactivations and internal transfers.
Total C.Ps / Net C.P GrowthUse distinct worker identities and an agreed active-employment rule. Cross-office working must not duplicate the group headcount. Home office and deployment office are different concepts.
Retention and TurnoverSplit into separate metrics. Turnover commonly uses leavers ÷ average active headcount and is lower-is-better; retention follows a starting cohort and is higher-is-better. Agree periods and inclusion rules.
Applications / Offers / HiresDistinguish applications from unique candidates and accepted offers from actual starters. Multiple applications from one person must not inflate worker headcount.
Absence / Sickness / Annual LeaveSeparate categories and their denominators. Desired hours alone are not a reliable universal absence denominator. Decide scheduled/contractual eligible hours, paid status and treatment of leave.
Capacity Utilisation / Scheduling EfficiencyDefine available billable capacity and distinguish planned from delivered care. Travel share, roster coverage and paid-time utilisation are separate measures; “efficiency” needs a chosen formula.
Predicted versus Actual GH wasteKeep forecast based on planned work separate from realised waste based on verified eligible work and pay policy. Credit cross-office work back to the worker’s home office. Cost needs an approved rate and treatment of paid leave/training; unused hours alone are not a complete cost calculation.
Training ComplianceChoose in-date completed mandatory assignments ÷ required/due assignments, or fully compliant staff ÷ staff in scope. These are different metrics. Group percentages must be recalculated from their components.
Support Visits / Supervisions / AppraisalsCounts are useful activity measures, but due/on-time completion rates are better compliance indicators. Define who is due, frequency and exclusions.
Gross / Net Profit and Salary %Finance must own chart-of-accounts mapping, adjustments and cost allocation. Use approved consolidation/eliminations for the group; do not simply sum entity profit figures and call them consolidated profit.
Complaints / ReviewsPair complaint counts with severity, exposure and resolution performance. Pair review score with review count and platform. Do not average office ratings equally or combine different platforms without a defined method.
Visa expiry / SponsorshipThese are restricted workforce records, not standalone general-access KPIs. A useful aggregate is cases due within 30/60/90 days and overdue checks, with authorised case-level drill-down only.
Aggregation rule: revenue and hours usually sum; group headcount needs distinct identities; percentages normally require combined numerators and denominators; a period-end snapshot is not summed over weeks; text reasons are categorical distributions, not numeric totals. Missing and not-applicable must never be treated as zero.

The existing weekly grid also stores monthly revenue information. If monthly snapshots are repeated in weekly records, summing those weekly rows would double-count monthly revenue. Establish one authoritative entity-and-period result and separate snapshots from movements.

3. Five core streams — plus explicit gaps

Agreed core scope: Xero finance through the existing Joiin reporting route where suitable; Zoho CRM; People Planner/Care Capacity; Jobtrain; Lumis. Zoho Books is not used and is out of scope. Joiin is the existing finance consolidation/reporting layer, not a sixth operational business system.

Not automatically covered by those five streams

  • Payroll and HR: exact wage costs, employment history, visa/right-to-work records and sponsorship may require approved payroll/HR data or controlled manual imports.
  • Public review platforms: Homecare.co.uk, Google and Trustist are additional feeds. Prefer approved APIs, authorised exports or an agreed aggregator. “Web page potential” is not permission to scrape or proof of access.
  • Actual care outcomes: completed/missed/late visits require reliable attendance/outcome data; a schedule alone proves only the plan.
  • Quality and safeguarding: complaint classifications, incidents and action outcomes may need task/event exports or a separate governed input process.

Vendor/source checklist

For each source, obtain: account/package owner; read-only access method; exact fields and example payload/report; stable IDs; all entity coverage; oldest available history; timezone/date semantics; pagination and rate limits; retries and change detection; deletion/correction behaviour; service terms and costs; and a small approved sample for reconciliation. Public API availability alone does not close this research.

5. Different teams need different views

Board: approximately 12–15 approved headline indicators, targets, trends, material exceptions and action summaries. Proposed areas: revenue/budget; profit margin; cash/debt risk where available; care hours; net client growth; enquiry conversion; demand/capacity gap; workforce growth/retention; GH waste; training compliance; material quality risk; and reporting completeness.

Heads of Department: full functional KPI catalogue, comparable office results, distribution, assurance queue, recurring causes and action ownership.

Local Managers: office scorecard, forward capacity risks, due submissions, exceptions and recovery actions. KPI delegates: only assigned measures and office scopes, with a work queue.

Retain the specification’s separation between working commentary and approved Board commentary. Do not present individual candidates, sickness details, visa documents or unrestricted staff training histories on the general Board dashboard. Administrative privileges should not automatically grant unrestricted business-data access.

Root-cause choices currently focus on Capacity, Enquiry, Both, Other and Not identified. Expand governed cause categories by function—for example recruitment delay, data quality, training expiry, cost movement and client churn—without collecting unnecessary sensitive details.

6. Recommended architecture: extend SUR BI, isolate the reporting work

Keep the existing SUR BI user experience and shared login initially. Do not create a competing application simply because there are more sources. Separate the reporting modules and background integration jobs from interactive care scheduling, so reporting failures or heavy syncs do not block operational work.

  1. Source ingestion: Hetzner/server-side connectors, existing People Planner reports, and controlled manual submissions/imports. Secrets and OAuth tokens stay on the server, with access controls and encryption; not in the browser.
  2. Master data: map source organisation IDs to legal entities, operating offices, departments and the group. Approximately 21 Xero organisations must not be assumed to equal the current 10 BI offices. Allow one-to-many mappings and effective dates.
  3. Normalised reporting facts: store dated events, period values and ratio components with source lineage. Use stable source IDs and idempotent upserts; repeated imports must not duplicate rows.
  4. Governed calculation layer: versioned metric definitions, targets and applicability; correct group aggregation; approved calendar; reconciliation and freshness rules.
  5. Performance workflow: review/approval, commentary, recovery actions, evidence controls and frozen published periods with governed corrections.
  6. Role-specific reporting: Board, Operations, departmental, local and delegate views, with server-side office/department/KPI authorization applied equally to APIs and exports.

Preserve the current database and ingestion routes unless there is a separately justified and approved change. Dedicated reporting tables/schema and jobs may be sufficient initially; decide whether a separate reporting database is needed after measuring actual volume and operational load, not simply the platform count.

Operational reliability

  • Use pagination, bounded batches, provider-wide rate budgets, retry/backoff and saved checkpoints. Avoid a full historical sync on a dashboard request.
  • Track each run’s scope, received/expected coverage, validation failures and completion state. Partial data must not silently publish as a complete group result.
  • Expose last successful sync, source period, data completeness and stale status. The last approved value can remain visible during an outage, clearly labelled.
  • Keep weekly operations and monthly closed finance as distinct reporting calendars. Near-real-time reflection of approval does not mean every vendor source refreshes every 15 minutes.
  • Validate retention, vendor agreements, legal basis, restricted employee/candidate fields and recovery arrangements with information governance. Retain only necessary source detail; do not log credentials or sensitive record contents.

Vendor-access research can run in parallel; metric and entity agreement must precede final calculation design. Do not wait for every API to deliver initial value: controlled imports can support a pilot, clearly labelled with their provenance and refresh status.

Ownership and estimating

Appoint a business sponsor, a product/operations owner, finance and functional KPI owners, technical/data engineering ownership and information-governance review. Integration access should have a named owner per supplier.

A defensible cost or delivery estimate requires vendor enablement/fees, required history, actual field samples, entity mapping, access personas, peak user/load assumptions and agreed pilot scope. This review does not invent a return-on-investment figure or a guaranteed implementation timescale.

First planning decisions

  1. Agree the small Board/pilot KPI set, rather than declaring 47 measures equally essential.
  2. Approve weekly/monthly calendars, “active client/worker” definitions and source-of-truth rules.
  3. Map legal entities, offices and all source identifiers before joining data.
  4. Obtain actual Joiin, Zoho CRM, Jobtrain and Lumis data-access samples and terms.
  5. Agree who can contribute, assure and publish, including rules for automatically imported results.
  6. Define reconciliation tolerances, refresh expectations and publication conditions.
