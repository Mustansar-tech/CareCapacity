# Breach Response Procedure

Article 33 requires notifying the ICO within 72 hours of becoming aware of a personal data breach likely to result in risk to individuals. Article 34 requires notifying affected individuals directly where the risk is high. This procedure exists so that clock can actually be met.

## Step 1 — Detect and Contain (immediately)
- Anyone who suspects a breach (unauthorised access, data leak, lost device, credential compromise, misdirected email, etc.) notifies Mustansar Hussain immediately — do not wait for confirmation.
- Contain first: rotate compromised credentials, revoke sessions, take affected systems offline if needed. Sentry and the audit log are the first places to check for scope.

## Step 2 — Assess (within 24 hours)
Answer, in writing:
1. What data was involved? Does it include special category data (PVG status, care-need data) or criminal-offence data?
2. How many data subjects are affected, and are any of them vulnerable adults?
3. Is the data still exposed, or has it been contained?
4. What is the likely risk to the affected individuals (identity theft, safeguarding risk, financial harm, distress)?

## Step 3 — Notify the ICO (within 72 hours of awareness, if risk threshold met)
- If the assessment concludes there is a risk to individuals' rights and freedoms, report via ico.org.uk within 72 hours of first becoming aware — even if the investigation is not complete. A phased report (initial + follow-up) is acceptable and expected practice.
- If genuinely no risk to individuals, document the reasoning and decision not to notify — this record itself is required under Art 33(5).

## Step 4 — Notify Affected Individuals (without undue delay, if high risk)
- Required where the breach is likely to result in a *high* risk (e.g. exposed PVG/safeguarding data, client care details, or credentials enabling account takeover).
- Notification must describe: what happened, what data was involved, likely consequences, and what SUR Group is doing about it, in plain language.

## Step 5 — Record and Review
- Every breach — reported to the ICO or not — is logged: date, nature, data/individuals affected, actions taken, decision on notification, and lessons learned.
- Review whether the DPIA, ROPA, or this procedure needs updating as a result.

## Who Decides
Mustansar Hussain leads the assessment and drafts the ICO notification, but final sign-off on ICO/individual notification rests with a company director — this is a business risk decision, not solely a technical one.
